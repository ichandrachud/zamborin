Ballast strategy: how to keep the bowl upright

Most people play Ballast as a merging game with an annoying bowl attached. That reading gets you a few thousand points and then stops working. The bowl is not an obstacle to the merging; the merging is how you control the bowl, and once you see it that way the game becomes a different and much better one.

Merging is not points, it is how you stay upright

The bowl rests on a rounded base, so it rocks. Whether it rights itself or keeps going depends on how high the weight sits, not only on which side you put it. A low pile sits below the point the curve can recover from and the bowl springs back. A high one sits above it and the same small nudge runs away.

Merging is the only thing that lowers the pile. Two orbs become one that is bigger but takes up less room than the pair did, and the surface drops. So a merge you make for the score is also a merge you make for your balance, and a run where you never tidy up is a run you will lose even if you never load one side.

This is the single most useful thing to understand. Everything below follows from it.

Read the ground, not the bowl

The coloured band under the bowl is the whole read-out. Green is the range the bowl comes back from on its own. Red is the range it does not. The white tick is not where the bowl is now, it is where your current load is taking it, so it moves before the lean does.

That is the point of it. By the time the bowl visibly leans you have already spent the drops that caused it. Watch the tick creep and act while it is still in green, and you will almost never lose a bowl you could have saved.

A high pile is more dangerous than a lopsided one

A modest lean with a low pile is nothing; the bowl carries it happily and rights itself the moment you rebalance. The same lean with a full bowl is close to fatal, because the weight is high and the curve has almost no authority left.

The practical consequence is that clearing a layer of small orbs buys more safety than it looks like it should. Small orbs are worth almost nothing on the scoreboard, which makes them easy to ignore, but every pair you clear takes height out of the pile and gives the base its recovery back.

Aim is a judgement, not a line

The claw shows where the orb is released and nothing more. There is no line down to where it will land, and that is deliberate, because that projection is the only sum the game asks you to do.

The orb falls straight down, but the release point belongs to the bowl and swings as the bowl leans. So when the bowl is tilted, the same aim lands somewhere different inside it. Lean right and everything drifts right of where you expect. Allow for it, and expect to be wrong for the first few drops of a session.

Recovering a lean

Once the tick is in the red the bowl is not going to right itself, and dropping on the high side is the only thing that helps. The demand grows fast, though: at a small lean you need the next orb a little to the high side, and by the time you are well over you need it further out than the bowl is wide.

So recovery is real but it has a window, and the window closes while you watch. Two or three drops spent purely on balance, taken early, will save a run. The same drops taken late will not.

The two largest cancel

Bring two of the biggest orbs together and both vanish for a large bonus. This is the release valve, and it matters more than the points: without it a strong run fills the bowl with orbs that have nowhere left to go, and the pile can only climb.

It is worth steering toward deliberately once you have two large orbs on the board, and it is worth accepting a slightly worse position to set up.

Small things worth knowing

  • A merge moves mass to the point where the two orbs met. That is a way to shift the balance without dropping anything, and it is the only one you have.
  • Small orbs are not filler. They settle into gaps and let large pairs find each other, which is what keeps cascades available.
  • The bowl is empty and heavy at the start, so early drops barely move it. That safety disappears as it fills; do not learn the game's forgiveness from its first minute.
  • A run can end two ways: the bowl rolls over, or an orb leaves it. They look nothing alike and the end card tells you which.
  • Every run is reproducible from its seed, so nothing is chance except the order of the orbs.

The short version

  • Keep the pile low. Height is what kills you.
  • Merge to survive, not to score. The points follow anyway.
  • Watch the tick, not the bowl. It warns you first.
  • Fix a lean while it is still in green.
  • Clear small orbs even though they are worth nothing.
  • Set up the big cancel before the bowl fills.
