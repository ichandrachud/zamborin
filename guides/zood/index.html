Zood strategy: how to aim, bank shots, and clear the board

Zood looks like you just point and shoot — but the players who clear the board are thinking two shots ahead, hunting for drops instead of matches, and quietly starving the board of colours. Here's what actually moves the needle.

Aiming: the cannon points where you point

Move your mouse (or drag your finger) and the cannon rotates to follow. The Zood fires from the tip of the barrel in a straight line, so where you're pointing is exactly where it goes — no gravity, no arc. One thing to know up front: the cannon can't aim flat sideways. It's capped at roughly 77° from vertical, so anything hugging the far edges of the board usually needs a bank shot rather than a direct line. Take the half-second to line up before you tap; a wasted shot isn't just a miss, it feeds the board.

Bank shots off the walls

A Zood bounces off the left and right walls at a clean, mirror-image angle — it doesn't lose speed. That's your tool for everything you can't hit head-on: gaps tucked into a corner, cells shadowed by an overhang, or a lone pair hiding up in the top rows. Picture the bounce as a straight line folded at the wall: aim at the wall so the reflected path lands where you want. On the wider desktop board you can even double-bank into deep pockets. When a direct shot looks impossible, the wall almost always opens it.

Match three — but chase the drop

The core rule: land three or more of the same Zood, all connected, and the whole group pops. Simple matches keep you alive, but they're not where the game is won. The real prize is the drop. Every Zood on the board hangs from the top row — anything that loses its connection to the top falls away instantly, and dropped Zoods are worth double a normal pop.

So stop thinking "what can I match?" and start thinking "what is holding up that big clump?" If a fat cluster is anchored to the ceiling by just two or three same-coloured Zoods, popping those anchors drops everything beneath them in one shot. That's how you clear a third of the board with a single well-placed Zood instead of chipping away three at a time.

Play the current and the next Zood

The Zood at the cannon's mouth is what fires now; the circled tile in the middle of the tray is what loads next. You always see two moves ahead, so use it. If your current Zood has nowhere useful to go, don't force a bad match — spend it setting up a spot where next Zood lands for a big pop or drop. Two average shots played in sequence beat one lucky shot every time.

There's a quieter rule underneath this that's worth knowing: the game only ever hands you colours that are still on the board. Clear a colour off completely and it stops appearing in your tray for good. That means every full colour you eliminate makes your remaining shots more predictable — fewer colours in the mix, easier matches. When you're near the end, deliberately wiping out one colour at a time snowballs into a much easier finish.

Keep the cluster high — mind the miss counter

The board doesn't creep down every turn. It descends on a counter: five shots that clear nothing and the entire cluster drops a row while a fresh row of Zoods appears at the top. Note that word — nothing. A shot that pops even a single group of three resets you; only true whiffs count toward the descent. So when the board is getting tall, the priority isn't points, it's connecting on every shot. A guaranteed three-match you don't love still buys you breathing room.

You lose the instant any Zood crosses the danger line just above the cannon. If the cluster is low and threatening, drop the ambitions: take the safe pop, keep the counter clean, and rebuild from there.

When to clear vs. when to build

Early, with the cluster sitting high and safe, you can afford to build — bank a Zood into a corner to fatten a group, or park one next to a big overhang so your next shot triggers a huge drop. That's when you rack up points. But the moment the board grows past comfortable, flip to clear mode: every shot connects, every miss is a real cost. Knowing which mode you're in is most of the skill.

Quick checklist

  • Line up before firing — a miss feeds the board.
  • Use bank shots for corners and anything past the cannon's angle limit.
  • Hunt for anchors holding up big clumps; drops beat matches and score double.
  • Read the next Zood and play two shots as one plan.
  • Wipe out whole colours to thin the pool late-game.
  • When the board is tall, connect on every shot — five clean misses and it descends.
