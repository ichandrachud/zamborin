Bloom strategy: how to route the water and open every flower

Bloom never asks you to guess. The water always shows exactly how far it has reached, so solving a board is about reading the tangle and turning the pipes in the right order. Here is how to think about it.

Start at the tap and follow the water

Water only ever comes from one place: the tap in the middle of the garden. Every pipe carrying water turns blue, so at any moment you can see the exact edge of the flow. The single most useful habit is to always work from that blue edge outward. Find the last watered pipe, then turn the dry piece just beyond it so the flow can continue. Chasing pipes far out in a dry corner is wasted effort until the water has a route to them.

Fix the pipes nearest the tap first

Everything downstream depends on the pipes closest to the source. If a junction near the tap is pointing the wrong way, nothing beyond it can ever fill, no matter how neatly you arrange the far pipes. Lock in the connections near the tap early, watch the blue spread a little further, then move outward one ring at a time. Solving Bloom is really a wave moving out from the centre.

Read the junctions

Straight pipes and elbows only pass water along, but tees and crosses branch it. Those are the pieces that decide how the water splits across the garden. When two flowers sit on different branches, a well-aimed junction can feed both at once. Before turning a junction, look at what sits on each of its arms, and choose the orientation that sends water toward the branches that still need it, not back the way it came.

When a branch stays dry

If part of the garden refuses to fill, trace back from the closed flower toward the tap and find the first pipe that is still grey. That grey piece, sitting right where the blue stops, is almost always the one to turn. Bloom rarely requires a clever leap. It requires patience and following the water back to the exact spot where the chain breaks.

Think a turn ahead on tight boards

As the garden grows, one route can steal water that another branch needs, so the order you solve things in starts to matter. If feeding one flower leaves another stranded, that is a sign to rearrange an upstream junction rather than force the last connection. Use Undo freely to test a route, and Restart if a board gets away from you. Nothing is lost, and every board has a way through.

Quick checklist

  • Water comes only from the tap. Blue means watered, grey means dry.
  • Always turn the grey pipe sitting just past the blue edge.
  • Fix the pipes nearest the tap first. Everything downstream depends on them.
  • Aim junctions to feed the branches that still need water.
  • If a flower stays closed, trace back to the first grey pipe and turn it.
  • On knotted boards, rearrange upstream rather than forcing the final link.
