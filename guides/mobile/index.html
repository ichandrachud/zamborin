Mobile strategy: how to balance a Calder sculpture

Mobile has exactly one rule and the sculpture shows it to you continuously. Most people still get stuck in the same place: they hang the biggest shape on the longest arm because it feels generous. It is the wrong way round, and here is why.

One rule, and it is written on the sculpture

A rod hangs level when the weight on one side times the length of that arm equals the weight on the other side times its arm. Nothing else is going on. The pivots never move, so each rod is a fixed ratio you have to hit with the pieces you were given.

The consequence people miss: a heavy shape hung close in balances a light one hung far out. A short arm needs more weight, not less. If you keep reaching for the biggest piece to put on the long side, you have it backwards.

Weight is area, and area is judged by eye

The area of a shape is its weight. There is no number on it and there is not meant to be. Because area grows as the square of width, a piece that is 20% heavier is only about 9% wider, which is exactly why the game is worth playing slowly. Two shapes that look nearly the same size are not nearly the same weight.

Compare pieces against each other rather than in isolation. Hold one next to another in the tray and ask which is bigger, not how big either one is.

Work from the bottom up

What hangs from a rod end is everything below it, so the lowest rods settle first and then behave as a single lump for the rod above. Balance the deepest pair, treat that whole sub-mobile as one weight, and move up. Starting at the top means every change underneath undoes what you just did.

The droop is the answer, and it is honest

An unbalanced rod leans on its heavy side and keeps leaning until you fix it. That is not decoration, it is the sculpture showing you its own error, and it is proportional: a rod that is twice as wrong looks twice as wrong. Watch which end goes down and swap that piece for a lighter one, or lengthen the argument by moving weight on the other side.

Let it settle before you judge it. Everything sways together because every rod carries what hangs beneath it, and a rod that looks wrong mid-swing may be right.

The tray has decoys

You are given exactly the pieces the sculpture needs, plus spares that are deliberately close in size to the ones that fit. A piece that looks about right frequently is not. If a rod is stubbornly slightly off, the piece on it is probably a decoy one step away from the right one.

There is one answer, and no way to lose

Every board has exactly one arrangement that balances, and hanging pieces at random will not find it. There is no timer, no move count and no failure state. Take a piece off with a double click and try another. The whole game is the part where you stand back and look.

Quick checklist

  • Short arm, heavy piece. Long arm, light piece.
  • Balance the lowest rods first, then treat them as one weight.
  • Compare shapes against each other, never guess at one alone.
  • Remember 20% heavier is only about 9% wider.
  • Wait for it to settle before deciding a rod is wrong.
  • Whichever end droops is the heavy end. Lighten it, or load the other side.
  • If a rod is only slightly off, you are probably holding a decoy.

Hang the shapes until every arm sits level.
