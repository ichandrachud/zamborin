Tessera strategy: build longer words, guard the center, survive the speed-up

Tessera looks like a spelling game, but it's really a space-management puzzle with a dictionary attached. High scores come from a handful of habits — chasing length over quantity, thinking in two directions at once, and never letting the middle of the board choke. Here's what actually moves your score, tuned to how Tessera plays on Zamborin.

How a tile actually lands

One letter falls at a time, and it always appears in the center column. While it's dropping you steer it: on desktop the ← and → arrow keys slide it, ↓ or Space makes it rain down fast, and you can just click the column you want. On mobile you tap a column to send the tile there and double-tap to slam it home. Wherever you point it, the tile drops to the lowest empty cell in that column — you're stacking from the floor up, not placing it in mid-air.

That's the whole physical layer: pick a column, the letter settles on top of whatever's already there. The skill is deciding which column turns loose letters into words.

Length is everything — one long word beats three short ones

Scoring rewards length brutally: a word is worth its length squared. A three-letter word banks 90 points, but a four is 160, a five is 250, a six is 360, and a seven-letter word pays 490 — more than five short words combined. The lesson writes itself: don't reflexively cash in every three-letter word the instant it appears. If you can see a CAT growing into a CATS or a SCATTER, hold the shape and feed it. Words of three to seven letters count, and the dictionary is the full TWL06 Scrabble list, so plurals, odd little words, and obscure valids all score the same as everyday ones.

Think across AND down at the same time

Every placed tile sits at the crossing of a row and a column, and Tessera checks both directions when a tile locks. That's your biggest source of free points: a single well-chosen letter can complete a word going across and anchor another going down. When you're deciding where a vowel goes, don't just ask "does this finish my row?" — glance at the column too. The best players are constantly hunting for the tile that pays twice.

Because detection is greedy and takes the longest match at each spot, a five-letter word will clear as a five, not as the little three-letter word hiding inside it. Build the long shape with confidence; the game won't shortchange you by clearing the fragment early.

Guard the center column above all

Here's the rule that separates long runs from quick deaths: new tiles always spawn in the center column, and the game ends the moment that spawn cell is blocked. You can have empty space on the flanks and still lose if the middle is stacked to the top. So treat the center as sacred ground — clear words through it, keep its height low, and resist dumping awkward letters there just because it's convenient. When in doubt, park junk letters on the outer columns and keep the middle breathing.

Keep the stack low and let gravity work for you

When a word clears, its tiles vanish and everything above them drops down to fill the gap. That falling can quietly line up a brand-new word — but Tessera only re-checks the board when you lock your next tile, not automatically after gravity. So a word that gravity assembles for you will pay off the instant you place your following piece. Set these up on purpose: clear a low word, notice what dropped into place, and drop your next letter to trigger the bonus.

The broader habit is simply to clear often. Hoarding letters for one giant play feels clever, but a tall board gives you less room to steer and punishes a single unlucky run of consonants. Frequent small-to-medium clears keep you alive and keep your options open.

Keep your options open as it speeds up

Tessera starts gentle and accelerates the whole way — every letter you drop shaves a little off the fall time, so what felt calm early becomes a reflex test late. Two things carry you through. First, use the NEXT preview in the HUD: you always know the upcoming letter, so plan the current tile with the next one in mind. Second, the letter bag is streak-limited — you'll never get flooded with more than three vowels or three consonants in a row, so a workable letter is always close. Keep flexible letters like R, S, T and the vowels in open positions where they can join several possible words, and you'll rarely be stuck when the clock tightens.

Common mistakes

  • Cashing in every three-letter word on sight instead of growing it into a five or six.
  • Letting the center column creep upward — that's how most games end early.
  • Building only horizontally and ignoring the vertical word a tile could complete.
  • Fast-dropping on reflex and burying a letter you needed one column over.
  • Hoarding for a giant word until the stack is too tall to maneuver.

Quick checklist

  • Chase length — a seven-letter word outscores five short ones.
  • Check both directions before you lock a tile.
  • Keep the center column low; it's where you live or die.
  • Clear often to stay short and flexible.
  • Read the NEXT tile and plan one move ahead.
  • Set up gravity clears — the payoff lands on your next drop.
