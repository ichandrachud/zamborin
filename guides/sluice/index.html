Sluice strategy: the three tile move that solves everything

Most people bounce off Sluice at the same place. You get three flowers open, slide one more lane, and everything falls apart at once. That is not bad luck and it is not a difficulty spike. It means you are still solving it a lane at a time, and Sluice cannot be solved a lane at a time. Here is what to do instead.

Get the tap connected before anything else

Water enters the board at one square: whichever tile is currently sitting next to the tap, and only if that tile has a pipe end facing it. If it does not, nothing anywhere on the board is wet, no matter how beautifully the rest of it is arranged.

This is worth knowing because a dry board looks identical whether you are one move from finishing or thirty. Connect the tap first, and every move after that gives you information.

Read the blue, not the flowers

Flowers are a poor guide. They only open at the very end of a completed chain, so you can make three genuinely good moves in a row and see no flowers at all. The blue is the honest signal: it shows precisely how far the water has spread, and it responds to every single slide.

Watch it while you drag, too. The water updates as the lane moves, before you let go, so you can try a slide with your finger still down and take it back if the blue shrinks.

The move that changes almost nothing

This is the one that matters, and it is the reason Sluice has a Hint button that plays a sequence instead of pointing at a lane.

A row and a column cross at exactly one square. So do this:

  1. Slide a row one step.
  2. Slide a column one step.
  3. Slide that same row one step back.
  4. Slide that same column one step back.

It looks like you have undone your own work. You have not. The board is now different in exactly three squares, and every other square on the board is untouched. Four big lane movements, three tiles changed.

That is the whole game. It is what lets you repair one corner without demolishing the three flowers you already opened, and it is the same principle a Rubik's cube rests on: nobody solves a cube by searching, they learn a short sequence that moves a few pieces and leaves the rest alone.

Change which row, which column, or which direction, and you move a different three squares. Press Hint on a board you already half understand and watch which three end up ringed. Once you see it once, you have it.

Why you cannot solve it corner by corner

In an ordinary pipe puzzle each piece is independent, so you can lock in one region and move on. Sluice has no independent pieces. A row slide moves a tile out of every column it crosses, and a column slide moves a tile out of every row. Anything you "finish" is still exposed to everything you do next.

The practical consequence: build the long path first and fix the details last, using three tile sequences for the details. Trying it the other way round is exactly the loop where you open three flowers and lose them again.

Every board has one solution

Worth knowing when you are stuck: there is no second answer you might stumble into. Each level is checked when it is built, and any board with more than one arrangement that waters every flower is discarded and made again.

So a board that feels impossible is not underdetermined, and you are not missing a luckier route. There is one target and you are somewhere near it.

Quick checklist

  • Connect the tap first. A dry board tells you nothing.
  • Judge every move by the spread of blue, not by flowers opening.
  • Try slides with your finger still down and take back the ones that shrink the water.
  • Rough out the long route with plain slides.
  • Once the route roughly exists, switch to row, column, row back, column back for the repairs.
  • If you cannot see the sequence, press Hint and watch which three squares get ringed.
