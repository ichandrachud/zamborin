Pane: the rain on glass puzzle

Watch rain on a window for long enough and you see the rule. Drops sit still until two of them touch, and the moment they do they become one bigger drop, which now reaches further, and may touch another. Get one heavy enough and it breaks loose and runs, wandering down the glass and taking everything in its path with it.

Pane is that rule as a puzzle. You have a fogged window, a scattering of drops, and a fixed number of taps. Clear the glass.

How to play

Tap a drop and it swells. If swelling brings it into contact with a neighbour they merge instantly, and because the bigger drop reaches further, one tap can pull in a whole cluster. Keep feeding a drop and eventually it breaks loose and runs.

  • Feed a drop: tap it. It grows, and anything it now touches joins it.
  • Make it run: a heavy enough drop lets go and travels down the glass, clearing every drop it passes and leaving a clean track behind.
  • Watch your taps: the count is at the top. A tap that grows a drop which was never going to run is the one that costs you the level.
  • Undo: takes back one tap at a time, as far as you like.

Your progress saves automatically. There is no timer.

Why it is clever

A run does not just remove one drop, it removes everything along a path. So clearing the window is not about merging things, it is about choosing a small number of runs whose tracks between them cover the whole pane. That is a covering problem, and covering problems are exactly the ones where taking the biggest bite available each turn falls short.

That was measured before any of this was drawn. Four different ways of playing without thinking were tried against sixty levels of boards, including taking whatever clears the most and always feeding the biggest drop. On 87 percent of boards, every one of them failed even given a spare tap.

It is a quiet game. One tap takes about an eighth of the window, not half of it, and the pleasure is a clean track through the fog rather than a flood.

Tips

  • Work out which drop is going to run before you start feeding anything. Feeding the wrong one is how levels are lost.
  • A run travels downward, so a drop high on the glass is worth more than the same drop near the bottom.
  • Merging is free progress in mass but not in clearing. Only a run actually takes water off the window.
  • Runs are pulled toward other drops, so a cluster below your drop is a reason to let it go, not a problem.

Frequently asked questions

Is Pane free? Yes, completely free, with no account and no download, in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop.

Does it work on mobile? Yes. The window fills the screen and every drop is a full cell wide as a tap target.

How do I win? Every drop off the glass, inside your allowance of taps.

Is every level solvable? Yes. Each board is checked by an exhaustive solver when it is made, and you are given two taps more than the shortest answer needs.
