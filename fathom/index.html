Fathom: Only light things may rise

Pilot a submarine into a trench where the ocean itself has floors. Everything you mine makes you heavier, and only light things are allowed back up.

How to play

Your submarine obeys one law: heavy things sink and light things rise. Hold down to dive and up to rise; let go and the sub holds its depth. Rising spends the same air you breathe.

  • The verb: down to dive, up to rise, thrust left and right against the current. Touch a deposit and the claw takes it.
  • The feedback: the cargo bar counts kilograms, because every kilogram is a promise you will keep on the way up. If a layer will not let you through, the SEALED banner names the exact weight to drop.
  • The goal: reach the surface with the richest haul you can still lift. Banked money is safe forever.
  • DROP CARGO: sheds your heaviest item instantly. It always works.

Why it is clever

The ocean in Fathom has floors. Density layers only let heavy things sink through them and light things rise through them, and ore is weight. So every grab is a bargain with the trip home: nodules are heavy and cheap, crystals are light and precious, and a hold full of the wrong rock is an anchor. The layer that seals you in is drawn on screen the whole time, with the exact weight you would need to drop.

Tips

  • Read the dollars-per-kilogram, not the dollars. Grabbing everything is the losing strategy by design.
  • Resting on a layer costs nothing. Use the floors as base camps and plan the climb before you are heavy.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fathom free? Yes, completely free to play, with no account or download, in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop.

Does it work on mobile? Yes. Fathom is built for touch as well as mouse, with two control schemes to choose from.

How do I win? Surface with your haul: everything you carry up is banked, and only what you bank is safe.

Can I get stuck under a layer? No. Dropping cargo always sheds enough weight to rise, and the banner tells you exactly how many kilograms to drop.
