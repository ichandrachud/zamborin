Marble: roll it home

A chrome marble, glowing courses high above a neon city, and a ring of light at the end of each one. Marble is the first 3D game on Zamborin: two hundred levels, fifty across the neon city, fifty through Tokyo, fifty inside a pinball machine and fifty round a tin toy circus, each course harder than the one before; the pinball machine and the circus each start gentler, then climb again. The pinball machine and the circus each bring their own obstacles and puzzles, and each world has its own place behind the wormholes: the crystal canyon for the city and Tokyo, the moon playfield in the pinball machine, and in the circus a maze of mirrors. From level 2 a puzzle square stands before every finish, and its way out stays shut until you work it out. From level 41 two more puzzles stand on the road before it.

How to play

Put a finger anywhere on the course and drag. The marble rolls the way you drag, and the further you drag, the harder it rolls.

  • Steer: drag on a phone, or drag with the mouse or use the arrow keys on a computer.
  • Save your place: roll through a blue ring and it turns green. That is where you start again.
  • Fall off: the marble flies back to the last green ring, and the fall is counted.
  • Finish: roll through the orange ring at the end of the course.
  • Puzzle squares: from level 2 the finish stands past a walled square whose way out is shut. As you roll up to it the camera rises to show the whole square: look before you move. Stuck? Roll over the reset pad beside the road in, and the square goes back as it was.
  • Keys: roll over a key to take it. You carry one at a time, so taking another leaves the one you held in its place. A gate opens for the key of its colour and shape, and keeps it, so count your keys before you open a gate.
  • Switches: a switch flips every gate with its letter, shut to open and open to shut, so it can shut the way you came. Roll over it again to flip them back.
  • Crates and plates: a plate holds its gates open while something heavy is on it. Roll into a crate to push it one cell. A crate can't be pulled and never goes through a gate.
  • Turning bridges: a lever turns every bridge with its letter a quarter turn clockwise. A bridge is open only where its lit road meets the side; railings close the rest.
  • Charge: a bolt pad charges the marble and a ground pad empties it. Lightning gates let only a charged marble through, and floors marked with plus signs push a charged marble away, the way like charges do.
  • Light: a lamp shines a beam across the square, over the walls. Roll under a mirror to turn it. A gate of light is open only while the beam reaches the crystal.
  • Stars: each level has a star time, shown beside the star at the bottom of the screen. Finish under it to win the level's star.
  • Obstacles: neon bollards, road-works barriers and cargo crates stand in rows across some roads, with one gap in each row. They're solid: steer through the gaps. A hard knock near the edge can throw the marble off the road.
  • Traffic: flying cars cross some roads. Wait at the line for the green light, then roll across. A car knocks the marble off the road.
  • Power switches: a dark road cannot be crossed. A line of lamps leads from it down a side road to a switch. Roll over the switch and the road lights up.
  • Moving pads: pads with a blue grid carry you across gaps. Wait for one to stop at your end, roll on, and roll off when it stops at the other.
  • Wormholes: a swirling ring of light on the road takes the marble to another world, and the way out at its far end brings you back on the far side of a gap nothing else can cross. Over the city and through Tokyo it is the crystal canyon, a valley of glowing yellow and red crystals at night. Its green crystal road is slippery, so the marble slides: brake early. In the canyon, crystals grow up out of the road, fire jets flare in a rhythm, fireballs roll across, and crystal bridges crack under you and fall away, so never stop on them. In the pinball machine it is the moon playfield, just as slippery, with volcanic vents and lava bombs.
  • The mirror maze: in the circus a magician's cabinet takes the marble into a maze of mirrors, a different one in each level. Every mirror shows another corridor, and some doorways are clear glass: bump one and it cracks, so you know it next time. The view comes down behind the marble and turns with it, and dragging up still rolls the way you face. The way out is the cabinet at the far end.
  • Forks: where the road splits, the narrow way is quicker and the wide way is safer. Both lead on.
  • Loop-de-loops: roll over the yellow arrows before a loop, and it carries the marble all the way round. Too slow, and the marble rolls back to try again.
  • Maglev strips: cyan arrows on the road pull the marble toward the edge they point to. Steer the other way to stay on.
  • The sky train: it stops at stations. Roll onto the deck on its roof, hold on as it pulls away (the marble rolls back as it speeds up), and roll off at the next station.
  • Hologram bridges: see-through bridges switch off and on. Cross while they are lit. They flicker just before they go dark.
  • Speed strips and jump pads: yellow arrows speed the marble up, and yellow rings throw it into the air, over the gap ahead.
  • Colour locks: a lime or violet wall lets through only a marble of its own colour. The road forks first into a lime lane and a violet lane, and each colours the marble that rolls through it. Look at the wall, then pick the lane.
  • Roundabouts: a ring road turns round a glowing island and carries the marble round with it. Roads leave it on three sides, and only one leads on; the others stop short at a red bar. Roll off onto the one that leads on.
  • Wind between the towers: gusts blow out of the gap between two towers and push the marble across the road, even in the air. Streaks of light and a rising whoosh come just before each gust.
  • Scanner lasers: a red scanner bar sweeps across the road, and touching it sends the marble back to the last ring. Roll down one side just after the bar has left it. Where two bars sweep, go down the middle just after they cross.
  • Glass tubes: roll into the mouth of a clear tube, and it carries the marble up, round a coil high over the city, and down onto the road ahead. Nothing to steer: enjoy the ride.
  • Puzzles on the road: from level 41 two more puzzles stand on the road before the square, eight kinds in turn: ice mazes where the marble slides until something stops it, crates to push into gaps to build a road, tiles that crumble behind you until every one is lit, a tune of drums to play back, a twin that moves as your mirror, tanks to fill so a boat can carry you over, planks to hop across a river, and picture riddles. Each one says what it wants the first time you meet it.
  • Tokyo: levels 51 to 100. Every piece keeps its rules and takes a Japanese form: red paper lanterns for bollards, level crossings for the flying cars, paper screens for the hologram roads, torii for the wormholes, a revolving stage for the roundabouts, carp streamers in the wind and taiko drums for the jump pads.
  • The pinball machine: levels 101 to 150. The city's pieces take the machine's form: meteor showers for the traffic, a shuttle for the sky train, ion streams for the maglev strips, orbit rings for the roundabouts, thrusters for the wind and a saucer's tractor beam for the scanner. Its own obstacles: ride round a black hole's rim, never across it; go through docking clamps just after they open; pass a magnet while it is dark; slip past a droid on the side it is not guarding; keep out of the glowing rings where lava bombs land. Its three puzzles in each level are its own: constellations, refuel and launch, wormholes between islands, satellites that glide until something stops them, and airlock codes.
  • The circus: levels 151 to 200. Roll through the middle of a ring of fire, never the flames. Pass the juggler just after a spot's ball has gone up. Reach the knife wheel as a hole comes to the bottom. Ride the trapeze from edge to edge, and keep to the middle of the high wire. Roll into the human cannon as it points at the net. Change pace after each ball the clown throws, and outrun the clown car. At the Ferris wheel, wait on the yellow spot and roll into a car only while the wheel stands still. On the teeterboard and the Wheel of Death, wait on the yellow spot and the act carries you over. At the roller coaster, wait at the station for a car, roll in and hold on. Its three puzzles in each level are its own: the clown car, the acrobat pyramid, the balance scales, the shell game, the knife thrower, ticket turnstiles and a house of mirrors.

Your level is saved, so you always pick up where you left off.

Tips

  • Short drags steer gently. Save the long drags for the straights.
  • A moving pad waits at each end of its run. Roll on while it waits, and stay in the middle while it moves.
  • Wait at the edge of a dark bridge. The moment it lights, go, and keep going.
  • After a speed strip, ease off before the next turn.
  • On the train, move to the middle of the deck before it leaves.
  • In the crystal canyon, let go well before a turn. The marble keeps sliding.
  • Took the wrong lane at a colour lock? Go back up the other one: it changes your colour.
  • On a maglev strip, start steering against the arrows as you roll on, not after the marble has moved.
  • Wind comes in gusts. Wait for one to pass, then cross, or lean into the streaks as they come.
  • On a roundabout, aim for the road out and start rolling toward it a little before it comes round.
  • At a scanner, wait at the red line and watch the bar go by once before you roll.
  • Among obstacles, line up with each gap before you reach it and roll through it straight.
  • At a puzzle square, stop at the door and look before you roll: what does each gate need, and where is it?
  • Every pad works each time you roll onto it: a switch flips again, a mirror turns again.
  • Wedged a crate in a corner? The reset pad by the road in puts every crate back.
  • Chasing a star? Take the narrow way at forks, and keep your speed on the straights.
  • On the moon playfield, as in the canyon, let go well before a turn.
  • In the circus, a yellow spot is a place to wait. Stop on it and watch the act once before you move.
  • Lost in the mirror maze? Keep one wall on the same side of the marble and follow it. It always leads to the way out.

Frequently asked questions

Is Marble free? Yes, completely free to play, with no account or download, in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop.

Does it work on mobile? Yes. Marble is built for touch first.

How many levels are there? Two hundred, in twenty-one districts. The neon city has the first fifty. Downtown has turns, narrow paths, ramps, neon bollards, road barriers, traffic crossings, power switches and forks in the road. Transit adds cargo crates, pads that move, wormholes to the crystal canyon, the sky train and maglev strips. The Holograms district adds bridges that switch off and on, roundabouts, colour locks and wind between the towers. Boost adds speed strips, jump pads, loop-de-loops, glass tubes and scanner lasers. The Express puts all of them together on the longest courses, and the Skyline, levels 41 to 50, is harder again. Levels 51 to 100 are Tokyo, in five districts: Shibuya, Akihabara, Asakusa, Shinjuku and Tokyo Tower. Every piece keeps its rules and takes a Japanese form there: red paper lanterns for bollards, level crossings with a little tram line, paper screens for the hologram roads, torii for the wormholes, a revolving stage for the roundabouts, carp streamers in the wind and taiko drums for the jump pads. Every level is harder than the one before, all the way to 100. From level 2 every course ends in a puzzle square: keys from level 2, switches from 9, crates from 14, turning bridges from 18, charge from 24 and light from 30; from level 36 they are mixed, and from 41 the squares grow bigger and harder. From level 41 two more puzzles stand on the road in every level, eight kinds in turn: ice mazes where the marble slides until something stops it, crates to push into gaps to build a road, tiles that crumble behind you until every one is lit, a tune of drums to play back, a twin that moves as your mirror, tanks to fill a channel exactly so a boat can carry you over, planks to hop across a river, and picture riddles. Each kind comes back harder every four levels. Levels 101 to 150 run through a pinball machine with a space theme, in five districts: Launch Lane, Ion Drift, Droid Dock, Moon Base and Hyperspace. It starts gentler than the end of Tokyo and climbs again to 150, every piece dressed as the machine's own, and it brings obstacles of its own: black holes that pull you toward their drain, docking clamps that snap shut, magnets that grab you and fling you sideways, droids that guard the rail and a volcano that lobs lava bombs. Its three puzzles in every level are its own too: constellations where rolling onto a star flips it and every star joined to it, a leaking fuel tank to fill before a launch pad throws you over a gap, wormholes between islands of floor, satellites that glide until something stops them, and airlock codes to key in. Levels 151 to 200 run round a tin toy circus, in five districts: The Big Top, High Above, Sideshow Alley, The Midway and the Grand Finale. It starts gentler again and climbs to 200, every piece dressed as the circus's own (juggling pins, clown cars, magician's cabinets, carousels), and it brings its acts: rings of fire, a juggler, a knife wheel, the trapeze, the high wire, a human cannon, a clown who throws balls at you, a house of mirrors, a clown car that chases you, a Ferris wheel over a broken rail, a teeterboard, the Wheel of Death and a roller coaster. Its three puzzles in every level are its own too: clowns to push into their car through its back door, acrobats to push onto the stars of a pyramid, weights to balance on a scale, the shell game, a knife thrower whose targets you must remember, ticket turnstiles with never enough tickets for all of them, and a house of mirrors to find your way out of. Its magician's cabinets lead into a maze of mirrors made for each level, where every mirror shows another corridor. Every level also has a star time: finish under it for the level's star.
