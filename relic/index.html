Relic: read the colours, rebuild the figure

A figure is broken into fragments and scattered. Colour bands run right through it, so every broken edge carries a sequence of colours. Put the fragments back so the bands run unbroken.

How to play

Fragments sit in the tray below the figure, turned the wrong way round. Drag one into a gap and it will sit there. Whether it belongs there is a separate question, and the game will not answer it for you. The answer is in the colours.

  • Drag: a fragment from the tray into any gap in the figure. It seats wherever you drop it, right or wrong.
  • Tap: a fragment, in the tray or already placed, to turn it a quarter turn.
  • The feedback: the bands themselves. Where a fragment belongs, the bands running through it carry straight on into its neighbours. Where it does not, they cut across at an angle, and it is obvious.
  • The goal: every gap filled, with every fragment the right way round.
  • Undo and Restart: both always free. Nothing is timed and nothing is lost.

Your level is saved automatically, so you always pick up where you left off.

Why it is clever

Every fragment is cut to exactly the same shape, and every one of them fits every gap in all four turns. That is deliberate. In an ordinary jigsaw the outline of a piece tells you where it goes, so the puzzle is solved by trying pieces rather than by thinking. Here the shape tells you nothing at all, and the only thing that can tell you where a fragment belongs is the sequence of colours along its broken edge. You can read an edge and know where a piece goes before you touch it.

Because the fragments come from one continuous figure, a solution always exists: the figure it was cut from. Nothing is ever removed that cannot go back.

Tips

  • Start at a gap with the most neighbours already in place. The more edges a gap has, the fewer fragments can possibly satisfy all of them.
  • Read the order of the colours across an edge, not just which colours are present. A fragment turned the wrong way round often shows the same colours in the wrong order.
  • Watch where a band crosses an edge, not only that it does. The bands curve, so the height and the angle at which one meets a break are what separate one gap from its neighbour.
  • The mistake a new player makes is dropping fragments in to see whether they stick. They always stick. Look before you drop.

Frequently asked questions

Is Relic free? Yes, completely free to play, with no account or download, in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop.

Does it work on mobile? Yes. Relic is built for touch as well as mouse, with its own portrait layout rather than a shrunken desktop one.

How do I win? Fill every gap with the fragment that belongs in it, the right way round, so the colour bands run unbroken across the whole figure.

Is every level solvable? Yes. Every level is one figure cut apart and put back, so the arrangement you are looking for is the one it started in.

Can I put a fragment in the wrong place? Yes, and that is the point. Nothing stops you and nothing warns you. The colours are the only feedback there is.
