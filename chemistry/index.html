Lessons in Chemistry: Hands that touch, clasp

Free radicals drift in a dish, every free hand ready to grab. Drag atoms in and move them around to build the molecules on the list, and steer clear of the ones that would grab them first.

How to play

Every atom has hands. Hydrogen, chlorine and sodium have one, oxygen, magnesium and calcium two, and iron three. Free radicals drift slowly around the dish, and every free hand grabs the first free hand that comes within reach.

  • The verb: drag an atom in from the Available panel, or drag anything already in the dish somewhere else.
  • The risk: whatever you carry grabs any radical it passes near, all the way along the drag, not only where you let go.
  • The feedback: hands stretch toward each other before they meet. A finished molecule on the list flies to the top. Anything else you finish stays in the dish as waste.
  • The goal: make every molecule on the list. Lose one and you can play on, but the level has to be done again.

Why it is clever

The atoms you need are in the dish and in the panel, and there are just enough of them. A hydrogen that grabs the chlorine meant for the iron makes hydrogen chloride, and the iron chloride can no longer be made.

Tips

  • Plan the route before you drag. The straight line is not always the safe one.
  • Move a troublesome radical out of the way first, slowly, through open space.

Frequently asked questions

Is Lessons in Chemistry free? Yes, completely free to play, with no account or download, in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop.

Does it work on mobile? Yes. It is built for touch as well as mouse.

How do I win? Make every molecule shown at the top.

Is every level solvable? Yes. Every level is checked by building every molecule from the atoms it starts with.
